# 48V10+10A ORing: dual USB-C PD EPR combined power board

Two USB-C PD EPR inputs (each 48V 5A, 240W) are combined through ideal-diode ORing into a single 48V output (about 10A), with current sharing between the two inputs done through PD adjustable voltage (AVS). The original purpose was to replace the mains input of an ATX power supply with two USB-C chargers, and power a computer through a DC-ATX module.

> **This is an R&D prototype with no safety certification of any kind.** Read "Electrical safety" below first. Use at your own risk.

## Electrical safety

- **High current.** The output is 48V DC, up to about 10A (480W). Although 48V is generally considered within the safety extra-low voltage range, the current during a short circuit or a poor contact is enough to overheat wires and connectors and start a fire, or to cause arc burns.
- **Wires and connectors.** Use 16 AWG or thicker wire on the 48V side, and connectors rated for 10A or more. Do not plug or unplug the XT60 under load; it will arc and damage the connector.
- **A fuse is recommended.** Put a fuse of about 12-15A in series with the output.
- **Watch polarity and short circuits.** Check polarity before wiring. If the output is shorted, neither the on-board protection nor the charger protection is guaranteed to react in time.
- **Heat.** The MOSFETs and shunt resistors heat up at high current and need sufficient airflow. Do not run at full load for long periods unattended.
- **Disconnect the inputs before servicing.** On the LTC4359 version, a channel that is turned off is truly disconnected (no leakage measured), but the output capacitors may still be charged. Unplug both USB-C inputs before servicing or wiring.
- **Voltage rating of input-side parts.** In the schematic, C28 on the VIN net is labeled 2.2µF/16V. It is not populated on the actual board; if you populate it, you must use a capacitor rated 63V or higher.
- **Charger protection.** The chargers used in testing protect themselves at about 6.4A sustained for about 80ms. If one channel drops out while the total load is above about 230W, the other channel is overloaded and the output cuts out immediately. When powering a computer, limit the system's power consumption so that the 48V side never exceeds about 7A in the worst case.

EasyEDA Pro (LCEDA) project, as a standalone file: `ProPrj_48V10+10A ORing_2026-10-10_en.epro2` (REV2, LTC4359 version).

In this copy the component library attributes (descriptions, categories, manufacturer names) and the schematic title block were translated from Chinese to English; the design data itself (parts, nets, footprints, board) is unchanged. The embedded preview images are regenerated by EasyEDA the next time the project is saved.

The project still contains the REV1 LM74700-Q1 sheets and parts. On REV1, the output of a turned-off channel still sits at about VIN − Vth and cannot be truly disconnected; treat the LTC4359 circuit as authoritative.

### MOSFET not yet changed

**This hardware project has not changed the MOSFET; it is still the IPT015N10N5.** Testing found:

- The IPT015N10N5 has a large gate charge (about 338nC for the pair), and when the gate drive current is small (the LTC4359 only provides 10µA, for example) it turns on very slowly. When load is applied suddenly from idle, the MOSFET stays in the linear region and goes outside its safe operating area. Two VIN-side MOSFETs were destroyed during testing.
- Recommended replacement: **IPT059N15N3ATMA1** (TOLL package, 150V, pin compatible), with a gate charge of about 69nC and a much larger safe operating area. In testing, the voltage dip on a cold 0→5A step dropped from 2.13V to 1.32V.

In addition, the actual board has the following modifications, none of which are in the project file:

- An SMCJ54A TVS and a 100V 100µF capacitor added on each input.
- A 1kΩ bleeder resistor on VOUT (about 48mA, 2.3W), which keeps the ideal diodes conducting at idle. Keep it even after changing the MOSFET.
- The gate protection Zeners D5 and D6 are DDZ9699T-7 in the project; the actual board uses BZT52C12VT (12V, SOD-523, cathode to INPUTx_GATE). Do not substitute 1N4148W; it has no forward clamp.

The firmware automatically detects both board types, REV1 (LM74700) and REV2 (LTC4359).

### Display and touch

The display and touch key are off-board modules, connected by ribbon cable to headers on the board:

|---|---|
| Display | GMT020-02-8P, 2.0 inch IPS, ST7789, 240×320, used by the firmware in landscape as 320×240. Module size 37.1 × 62 × 6 mm, viewing area 30.6 × 40.8 mm, 8P 2.54mm header |

## Build and flash

The firmware uses PlatformIO (`ch32v` platform).

```bash
cd MainCtrl
pio run -e MainCtrl_RELEASE -t upload
```

- The main controller is flashed with a WCH-LinkE (`upload_protocol = wlink`); only the sectors covered by the image are erased, so the settings area (the last 4KB of flash) is preserved.
- The X035 has no bootloader; flash the `.bin` in `X035Pd/output/` with WCH USB ISP.
- `MainCtrl/tools/v203ctl.py` can read status, switch channels on and off, and change the current-sharing mode over WCH-Link (for testing).

## Status and known limitations

Development has ended. The final test results are below (LTC4359 version board, IPT059N15N3, main controller firmware v0.0.103, X035Pd 2.2.3):

- Current sharing in MANUAL mode (learned feed-forward) passes 1↔8A slow ramp steps (0.1A/ms) ×10 and 1↔7A hard steps (10A/ms) ×10.
- 1↔8A hard steps fail. The limit comes from the chargers themselves: going from a PD voltage change to redistributed current takes at least about 130ms, which is slower than the chargers' self-protection at about 80ms; it is worse when the two channels' cable impedances differ.
- Cold steps from idle should be kept to about 5A or less (the rating of a single charger).

See `MainCtrl/docs/FLOW.md` for the detailed design history and the test records of each version.
