Q3 Breakdown Analysis

The CH1 VIN-side MOSFET (Q3, IPT015N10N5) has failed with a D-S short for the second time. This page collects the evidence, uses a calibrated step model to recompute the stress of each type of event, compares it against the datasheet SOA, and evaluates the effect of switching to the IPT059N15N3ATMA1.

Controller U1 LTC4359 Q3/Q1 IPT015N10N5 ×2 (back-to-back) VOUT bleeder 1 kΩ Input protection SMCJ54A + 100 µF
Conclusion

Answers to the two questions

Why did Q3 break down?
At turn-on it takes power beyond its SOA in the linear region. When VOUT is far below VIN, the LTC4359 charges the gate slowly with only 10 µA, and Q3 has to pass current across a 15–30 V drop for several ms.
  • The worst case is a V203 warm restart: with the 1 kΩ bleeder added, VOUT has already discharged to about 2 V after 1.4 s off, and when the path is turned back on Q3 uses about 85–109 % of SOA in a single event. During flashing and watchdog resets the charger was observed being pulled down to 36 V three times.
  • In the back-to-back arrangement only the VIN-side device must conduct "through its channel"; the VOUT-side device can conduct through its parasitic diode, so the failure is always on the VIN side (CH1 Q3 twice, CH2 VIN side once).
  • The IPT015N10N5 (OptiMOS 5) has a very weak SOA in the 20–30 V range: 30 V / 10 ms allows only 2.1 A.
Does switching to the IPT059N15N3ATMA1 fix it for good?
It greatly reduces the risk, but it is not a root fix. The same warm-restart event drops from 109 % to 19 %, about 5x margin; the trigger mechanism (slow gate, no inrush control) is unchanged.
  • SOA at 30 V: 52 A for 1 ms and 16 A for 10 ms, 6–8x the current part; gate charge 69 nC, about 2/5, so less time in the linear region; 150 V rating.
  • Cost: on-resistance 5.1 mΩ, about 0.26 W for the two devices per channel at 5 A (current part 0.07 W).
  • Combined with the immediate warm-restart recovery in v0.0.83 (VOUT dip < 0.25 V), the observed failure path is removed. A true root fix requires adding inrush control in the board revision.

Assessment: The part change brings the main event that has actually occurred from "beyond SOA" back to "about 1/5 of SOA", and since v0.0.83 has already eliminated warm-restart inrush, in practice this is enough to prevent the same failure from happening again. The trigger mechanism is still there, though: whenever VOUT is pulled down significantly during operation (hot-plugging a large capacitive load, recovering from an output short, a charger hiccuping repeatedly), Q3 will still turn on in the linear region. A root fix requires limiting inrush current in the board revision.
Countermeasures

Recommendations and verification checklist

Repair (this time)

  1. Replace Q3 with an IPT059N15N3ATMA1. Replace both devices of the same channel together (they share a gate, so Vth and Qg should match); replacing CH2 at the same time is also recommended.
  2. A Q3 breakdown often takes G-S and G-D with it: check D5 (12 V Zener) and R15 (10 Ω), and measure whether U1 GATE is about 10 V above SRC when powered.
  3. Unpowered measurement: D-S in diode mode about 0.5 V in one direction, OL in reverse; G-S tens of kΩ (R12 ∥ U1).
  4. Powered verification: CH1 ON, CH2 OFF, 0.3 A; VGS should be 10–12 V and VIN − VOUT only a few tens of mV.

Firmware and operation

  1. Done v0.0.83 immediate recovery on warm restart; v0.0.84 uses a firmware reset instead of wlink reset; v0.0.85 VIN LOST 25 ms.
  2. To do Keep latched faults across warm reset (this time PATH DROP was cleared by the IWDG reset and then automatically turned back on).
  3. To do Low-threshold OFF LEAK: with both channels off and no load, trip at > 20 mA (this time it was only 48 mA, which the 150 mA threshold could not catch).
  4. Keep cold-start steps ≤ 5 A; do not hot-plug large capacitive loads during operation (e.g. the input capacitors of an ATX power supply).
  5. When debugging use v203ctl reset, not wlink reset.

Board revision (root fix)

  1. Add inrush control: limit dV/dt or current at turn-on, e.g. an ideal diode controller with load-switch gate drive and settable dV/dt (something like the TI LM74800-Q1; specs need to be verified), or a hot-swap controller (with SOA timer).
  2. Or keep the LTC4359 and add a VOUT pre-charge path that brings VOUT close to VIN before turn-on.
  3. Choose MOSFETs rated for linear-region operation (e.g. series designed specifically for hot-swap), whose SOA curves are flat in the 20–40 V range.
